# data/

Lexical and morphological comparanda, assembled under
[`docs/preregistration.md`](../docs/preregistration.md) and its amendments
A1–A7 (§9). The comparison run on them is summarised in
[`../results/summary.md`](../results/summary.md). The two Proto-Afroasiatic
lists (A4–A7), the primary one and the sensitivity one, were assembled after
the Proto-Semitic arm had been run. They were then compared in the
Proto-Afroasiatic arm (mg-51839).

| file | contents |
|---|---|
| [`hattic.tsv`](hattic.tsv) | One row for each of the 100 Leipzig–Jakarta slots, with the Hattic form, stem and segmentation (§3.1), gloss as given, gloss confidence, provenance, and whether the form counts toward the decision (§1.2) |
| [`proto_semitic.tsv`](proto_semitic.tsv) | The same slots, with the Proto-Semitic root, the reconstruction and its level |
| [`proto_afroasiatic.tsv`](proto_afroasiatic.tsv) | The same slots for Proto-Afroasiatic, the second arm (A4, A5), with the reconstruction, its branches, the date viewed and a verbatim quote |
| [`proto_afroasiatic_hsed.tsv`](proto_afroasiatic_hsed.tsv) | The same slots for Proto-Afroasiatic from Orel & Stolbova 1995 (HSED) alone: the A4.1 sensitivity list, never decisive (A6, A7). Same columns as `proto_afroasiatic.tsv` |
| [`control_proto_uralic.tsv`](control_proto_uralic.tsv) | The same slots for Proto-Uralic, Control B (§4.2) |
| [`morphology.md`](morphology.md) | The eight paradigmatic items of §5.1 for all four languages, with testability as assembled |

Check the files with `python3 analysis/validate_data.py`.
`proto_afroasiatic.tsv` is generated by `analysis/paa_starling.py` (see
[`analysis/`](../analysis/)), which applies the A4.1 rules, as A5 reads them,
to the downloaded database. `proto_afroasiatic_hsed.tsv` is generated by
`analysis/paa_hsed.py`, which applies the same rules, as A6 restates them
for a printed book, to readings taken from the page images
(`analysis/paa_hsed_data.py`).

## Provenance

Every non-empty form carries a `provenance` value, which is one of:

- `cited:<work>, <page or entry>; <URL>`. The source text was **viewed during
  collection**, at that URL, and the form and gloss were read there
  (amendment A2). Internet Archive links use the page-image index
  `page/n<leaf>`. Forms from scanned books were checked against the page image
  as well as the OCR, because the OCR misreads ḫ, š, ɨ and similar letters.
- `uncollated`: entered without being checked against a viewed source.
  **No form in these files is uncollated.** Nothing was entered from memory.

Empty is empty. A slot that no viewed source fills is left blank, and the
`notes` column says what the source did give, if anything (for example a
lower-level Semitic form, or a Hattic word whose gloss is not the slot
meaning). Nothing was supplied to fill a gap.

## Columns

Columns common to all three TSVs: `slot`, `meaning` (as in the §1 table),
`strand` (`lexical`, or `morphology` for slots 9, 14, 35 and 56, which never
carry a form), `form`, `gloss_as_given` (the source's own gloss, often in
German), `match` (`exact` or `permitted-shift`, §2), `provenance` and `notes`.

The other columns are:

- **`hattic.tsv`**
  - `stem` and `segmentation`: the stem to compare and the analysis it rests on, with its source. `unsegmented` means no viewed source segments the word (§3.1).
  - `gloss_confidence`: `secure` or `doubtful`. A gloss is doubtful if the source marks it ("?", "vermutlich", "vielleicht", "unklar"…) or offers it only from context, such as a translation of a monolingual passage or a gloss known only through a compound.
  - `decision`: `counts` if the form is cited, secure and in a lexical slot (§1.2); otherwise `exploratory`.
- **`proto_semitic.tsv`**
  - `root`: the root as the source gives it.
  - `level`: always `Common Semitic` for a filled slot. The appendix's convention is that an unlabelled root is assuredly Common Semitic, and those rows quote that convention in their notes. Forms labelled West, Central, Northwest or East Semitic, or "Arabic root", are lower nodes; they do not fill a slot and are listed only in `notes` (A3).
- **`proto_afroasiatic.tsv`**
  - `root`: the reconstruction exactly as the database prints it, including any "(?)". `form` is the same without the "(?)", which is then recorded in `notes`.
  - `level`: always `Proto-Afro-Asiatic`, the database's own label.
  - `gloss_as_given`: the record's "Meaning" field. This is the gloss of the reconstruction itself. Daughter-reflex glosses are never used (A4.1).
  - `branches`: the branches that have a reflex field in the record, out of Semitic, Egyptian, Berber, Chadic, Cushitic and Omotic, with the database's subgroup fields in parentheses (A5 rule 4).
  - `non_semitic_branches`: how many of those are not Semitic. A4.1 reports the result again restricted to rows where this is at least 2.
  - `viewed`: the date the record was viewed, because the database can be revised (A4.1).
  - `quote`: "Proto-Afro-Asiatic: <reconstruction> Meaning: <gloss>", checked by machine to be a substring of the text of the cited page. On that page (`first=N`) the record is the first entry.
  - `notes`: doubt marks the source puts on the reconstruction, its gloss or its reflexes; how the tie-breakers chose the form; the record numbers of every other qualifying record; part-of-speech exclusions (A5 rule 3); and, for an empty slot, the near misses.
- **`proto_afroasiatic_hsed.tsv`**: the same columns, read as A6 states.
  - `root` and `form`: the heading reconstruction as printed, transcribed from the page image, not from the OCR. The OCR misreads most of them, for example `Yab-` for `*ˀab-`. HSED's raised hooks for the glottal stop and ʿayin are written ˀ and ᶜ.
  - `level`: always `Hamito-Semitic`, HSED's own label for the Proto-Afroasiatic level (A6 rule 5).
  - `gloss_as_given`: the gloss printed in the entry heading. A part-of-speech tag such as "(n.)" is not part of it, but it is kept in `quote`.
  - `provenance`: `cited:Orel & Stolbova 1995 (HSED), no. N, p. P; <scan>/page/n<leaf>`. The scan shows two pages per leaf, so p. P is on leaf 19 + P // 2. The validator checks this.
  - `branches`: A4.1's six branches, with HSED's own labels of the reflex lines in parentheses (WCh, CCh, ECh; Bed, Agaw, SA, LEC, Wrz, HEC, Dhl, Mgg, Rift). Only labelled reflex lines count. Forms named only in the commentary or after "Cf." do not count, and the notes say so where it matters (A6 rule 4).
  - `quote`: the heading verbatim, `N *reconstruction “gloss”`, read on the page image. It cannot be checked against the OCR by machine, because the OCR garbles it (A6 rule 7).
  - `notes`: the reflex glosses where they differ from the heading gloss, HSED's doubt marks and loan notes, how the tie-breakers chose the entry, the other entries they compared (with their branch counts), entries glossed with the slot meaning among other meanings, part-of-speech exclusions and, for an empty slot, the near misses.

### The two Proto-Afroasiatic lists compared

Both PAA lists fill 74 of the same slots. Only the primary list fills
slots 43 *black*, 45 *to stand*, 50 *what?*, 57 *good*, 78 *long* and
95 *small*. Only the HSED list fills slot 62 *to hear*. In HSED, most of the
missing slots have only predicate headings such as "be black", "be good",
"be long" and "be small". HSED glosses adjectives that way (p. XXVII). A6
keeps A5's reading, under which "be X" does not fill slot X. **For
information only:** if a leading "be" were ignored, 11 more HSED slots would
fill (28, 43, 54, 57, 72, 74, 77, 78, 90, 95, 99), and the Hattic overlap
would be 17. That reading is not registered and fills nothing. HSED also
leaves out pronouns, numerals, prepositions and particles (p. XXVII), so
*one*, *who?*, *what?*, *this* and *in* are empty.

One HSED slot turns on a label (A7): slot 40 *fish* is filled by entry 1085.
Entry 2061 would fill it if its Omotic line, labelled with the language
abbreviation "Ome", were counted. Slot 40 is not a Hattic slot.

### Sensitivity of the Hattic count

- **Strict reading of §1.1.** Soysal 2004, the governing source, could not be viewed, so 0 slots qualify.
- **As assembled (A3).** 17 slots qualify.
- **Kammenhuber's secure form instead of Schuster's doubtful one.** If Kammenhuber 1969's undoubted form were taken wherever Schuster 1974 offers only a doubtful one, the count would be 18 (slot 11, *to come*: Kammenhuber's nuu̯a „kommen, gehen“). The A3 precedence does not allow this. The figure is given only to show that the order of the two sources barely matters.

## What could not be sourced, plainly

- **No Hattic form was checked against Soysal 2004**, the source the
  pre-registration names as governing. Every Hattic form comes from two older
  works (1969, 1974), and a gloss they give may since have been revised.
- **No Proto-Semitic form was checked against Kogan 2011 or the SED.** All of
  them come from the third-precedence source, a dictionary appendix that lists
  only roots with English descendants. That is why 59 slots are empty.
- **Proto-Semitic morphology is almost empty** (2 of the 8 items), because the
  registered grammar could not be viewed. Only 1 of the 8 morphology items is
  testable for Hattic–Proto-Semitic as assembled; §5.3 needs 4.
- **Proto-Afroasiatic rests on one database.** It is the one A4.1 puts
  first. It has been sharply criticised, and its glosses are often broad
  ("go, run", "fire; burn"). Of the 80 PAA forms, 9 have reflexes in only one
  non-Semitic branch (A4.1, "Dependence on Semitic"). PAA morphology comes
  from the same database, because Hayward 2000 could not be viewed. Only
  3 of the 8 items are testable for Hattic–PAA.
- **The PAA sensitivity list comes from a 1995 book** that its own authors
  call "materials for a reconstruction". It has been widely criticised for
  loose semantic and phonological matching (A4.1). Of its 75 forms, 10 have
  reflexes in only one non-Semitic branch. HSED gives no morphology (it
  leaves out pronouns and particles), so the sensitivity run has no
  morphology strand. The OCR of the scan is unusable for the
  reconstructions, so every form was read on the page image. A misreading of
  a diacritic there is possible, and nothing checks it by machine.
- **72 of the 96 Hattic lexical slots are empty in both viewed sources.**
  This includes basic meanings such as *fire*, *water*, *eye*, *hand* and
  *mouth*.
